UNDER THE HOOD

The code's real, my dude.

The game looks chill as hell. The code under the hood? Not chill at all.

PuRlO's built from scratch in C++17 with SFML 2.6. No engine, no shortcuts. Every big system is hand-rolled.

Procedural tilemaps, steering-based enemy AI that orbits around you on its favourite flank, smooth camera follow, dual-sine screen shake, and a whole dialogue engine with typewriter text — all of it written just for this duck.

Each world rolls off seeded RNG, cluster-based terrain planting, random-walk path carving, and flood-fill checks so every single plant is actually grab-able. No dead ends, man.

Collision runs iterative least-penetration with per-axis sliding. Enemies keep their distance so they don't all pile onto you at once, recover from knockback, and check line of sight before they ever swing.

Enemy separation used to be naive all-pairs scans — O(n squared), twice a frame, across up to ~60 bones. Thousands of pair checks with nothing in between but a loop and hope. Now a spatial hash buckets everyone into a 128px grid each frame, so each bone only peeks at its own 3x3 hood. Same behaviour, cost drops from quadratic to basically-friends-only.

The dialogue engine is a hand-rolled word-wrap machine. SFML's immediate-mode and won't measure text for you, so every line builds a wrapped buffer by making a throwaway sf::Text per word just to size it, then types it out. Ugly, honest, and it works.

Window setup is genuinely cursed. SFML wants a video mode and style before the Game object's members even exist, so reading the fullscreen flag during construction is undefined behaviour. A function-local static pulls both values from the settings file once, before any member init touches them. There's a comment in the code apologizing for it at 4am.

Bones that get shoved into a wall by separation get walked back out by brute force: try every direction, smallest step first, until one's clear. One step per frame, only when actually stuck, so it can't tunnel through a thin wall. It ain't a navmesh. It is, however, load-bearing silliness.

Settings live in a plain ini-style file read into the sound system before the window even opens — resolution, volume, fullscreen. The menu writes globals, the game polls 'em every frame and rebuilds the window when they change. No callbacks. No events. Just globals and vibes.

The game also ships with a desktop editor: a Hammer-style map tool that paints tiles, places decorations and enemy spawns, and exports a tight binary .purlo file the game loads back verbatim. It's got 100 steps of undo that survive palette swaps, biome switches, even resizes, a live minimap, and a validate panel that flood-fills to make sure every plant's reachable before you hit Play.

FAQ

Ask the duck, man.

What are the controls?

WASD to move, Space to hop, Shift to sprint, K to dash, Left Ctrl to crouch (duck those slime balls), F to blast the shotgun, E to talk, I for the inventory. Escape closes menus. R restarts after you get dusted.

How does the dash work?

Dash sends you at 900 u/s for 0.2 seconds with a 0.6 second cooldown. You're invulnerable the whole ride and bonk bones on contact.

How many worlds are there?

Three: Grasslands, Desert, and Cave. Each one is 2800x2800 pixels of seeded procedural terrain with eight plants to snag.

What power-ups can I find?

Speed Potion gives 1.35x move speed for six seconds. Ghost Shield makes you immune for four. Hearts raise your max HP. And weed? That's the fog, man.

What is Fog Form?

You become a mist that drifts at 420 u/s, passes through walls and water, and can't take a scratch. No dashing, no hopping — just vibes.

Does inventory pause the game?

Nope, it's an overlay. Enemies keep moving and timers keep ticking while you decide what to pop.

Is PuRlO free?

Yup. Free and open source. Grab the Windows build or compile it yourself with CMake and SFML 2.6.

Was this made with a game engine?

Nope. C++17 plus SFML 2.6, all from scratch. Procedural generation, enemy AI, camera, dialogue, collision — all custom-built for the duck.
